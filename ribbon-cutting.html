We Cut the Ribbon

November 2026 · 3 min read · By James Kahle
WhenWednesday, November 4, 2026, 11 AM
WhereOuter Banks Chamber of Commerce, Kill Devil Hills
On the tableCookies and cold milk

Full Plate has been helping Outer Banks business owners since this summer, built on a belief I keep coming back to: business owners and their customers deserve a better experience. Last Wednesday we celebrated something that means just as much to me. We joined the Outer Banks Chamber of Commerce.

Joining the Chamber was about more than a membership. It was about putting down roots and standing alongside the businesses that make the Outer Banks what it is. So there was a banner, a red ribbon, a lobby full of people who took time out of their week to be there, and a pair of wooden scissors that needed both hands. I got to cut the ribbon. It took about one second. I am still smiling about it.

“Business owners and their customers deserve a better experience.”

The people who made it happen

A ribbon cutting is really a thank-you note you get to say out loud. Here is mine.

Melissa HarrisonChamber Board Chair

Melissa opened the morning with a welcome so warm it took the nerves right out of the room.

Hunter WalshChamber President & CEO

Hunter gave Full Plate this opportunity, and the Chamber he leads shows up for businesses here every single day.

Kelly AllmanChamber Team

Kelly organized every detail, from the run of show to the scissors, took every photo on this page, and made the whole thing feel easy.

Hannah ComerRight there in the middle of it

Hannah was standing beside me for the moment the ribbon came apart. Having her there turned a milestone into a memory.

And everyone who cameOn a Wednesday morning, no less

Some of you rearranged your day. Some of you drove across the bridge. All of you made it a celebration instead of a ceremony. Thank you.

What I said, in about a minute

The Chamber gave me a minute, and the cookies were waiting, so I kept it short. The gist:

I started Full Plate because I truly believe business owners and their customers deserve a better experience. I have spent my career working with startups, starting my own companies, helping others start theirs, and working for companies like Walmart, CVS and Amazon. Now I am taking everything I have learned and putting it to work for business owners right here on the Outer Banks.

That is the whole mission. Everything else is detail.

Marketing, served in order

If you looked at the banner behind us, you saw three stacked courses. That stack is how we work, and it is the part I most wanted people in the room to take home.

Growth
Customer Experience
The Foundations
Built from the bottom up.

A lot of businesses get served dessert first. Ads and posts show up before the basics are ready, and the owner wonders why the money went nowhere.

We start at the beginning. Get the foundations right: a website that works, a Google profile that is complete, a way to capture every lead. Then take great care of the customers you already have: quick replies, staying in touch, earning reviews. Then, and only then, grow.

Same order as a good meal. It just tastes better that way.

What comes next

The ribbon is cut and the cookies are gone. Now comes the part I actually started this for: helping Outer Banks business owners take great care of their customers, one plate at a time.

If you own a business here and want an honest look at where you stand, it starts with a free Kitchen Check. If you just want to say hi, our door is open, and so is the Chamber's. Joining the Outer Banks Chamber of Commerce was one of the best early decisions I made for Full Plate, and Wednesday was proof of why.

Thank you for being part of this.

James
Founder, Full Plate Marketing Co. · Proudly Outer Banks
